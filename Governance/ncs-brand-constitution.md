# Never Coming Soon
## Brand Constitution v1.1

## 1. The brand

Never Coming Soon imagines the best movies and television shows that do not exist.

We develop them as though they do.

Every edition introduces an original production and gives it enough life that a reader can picture the poster, hear the characters, see the scenes, argue about the casting, and wish someone would actually make the thing.

We are not writing about imaginary entertainment.

We are creating it.

## 2. Name and slogan

**Name:** Never Coming Soon

**Slogan:** The best movies and shows that don't exist.

## 3. Central conceit

Never Coming Soon is an imaginary entertainment studio whose productions exist on the page instead of the screen.

The studio can make anything:

- a huge summer blockbuster
- a tiny romantic comedy
- a ten-episode crime series
- a historical epic
- a broad comedy
- a genuine horror movie
- a family film
- a sports drama
- a quiet relationship story
- a superhero movie
- an animated adventure

The standard is not whether a real studio would make it.

The standard is whether we desperately wish it existed.

## 4. The core promise

Every edition should create some version of this reaction:

**Oh shit. I would actually watch this.**

That reaction cannot come only from a clever logline.

The reader should care about the people, understand the emotional promise, picture multiple scenes, enjoy the casting, and feel frustrated that the production is fictional.

## 5. What qualifies

A concept qualifies because it is worth wanting.

Sometimes it will be unusual.

Sometimes it will feel obvious in retrospect.

Sometimes the reaction should be, "No studio would ever make this."

Other times it should be, "How has nobody made this already?"

Unlikeliness is not a quality standard.

Realism is not a quality standard.

Novelty is not a quality standard by itself.

Desire is the standard.

## 6. The ordinary can be extraordinary

A Never Coming Soon concept does not need a gimmick.

A marriage drama can qualify because the people are extraordinary.

A romantic comedy can qualify because the chemistry is perfect.

A sports movie can qualify because the emotional story is irresistible.

A horror movie can begin from a familiar premise and become exceptional through execution.

A story can be quiet, sincere, or simple.

We should never mistake louder ideas for better ideas.

## 7. Audience

Never Coming Soon is built for mass culture.

The reader likes movies and television because movies and television are fun.

They send trailers to friends. They binge good shows. They remember great scenes. They argue about casting. They know when a premise sounds awesome.

Some readers will be cinephiles. Most will not be.

No specialist film knowledge should be required.

We share the audience's excitement. We do not lecture them.

## 8. Mass does not mean generic

Accessibility does not require obviousness.

Popular entertainment can be intelligent. Emotional work can be sophisticated. Comedy can be strange. Blockbusters can contain real characters. Quiet drama can reach millions.

Every production should be easy to enter and specific enough to remember.

## 9. Emotional center

Never Coming Soon exists because we love this stuff.

Passion should be visible through care, not declarations.

We care about:

- the villain's entrance
- whether the leads have chemistry
- the song that plays during the drive home
- the silence before someone answers the phone
- the episode everyone talks about the next morning
- the final shot
- the right actor rather than the most famous actor

The publication should feel like someone sharing something they cannot stop thinking about.

## 10. Genre integrity

Never Coming Soon has a personality. Its productions have their own personalities.

The publication never forces the same irony, humor, or cleverness onto every production.

A horror movie should sincerely try to scare.

A romance should care whether two people end up together.

A tragedy should be allowed to hurt.

A family movie can be earnest.

A comedy should actually be funny.

A sports movie should care about the competition.

A superhero movie should be allowed to think superheroes are cool.

We never make a genre while apologizing for liking it.

## 11. Relationship with Hollywood

Hollywood is part of the joke, not the enemy.

Never Coming Soon should not become a repetitive complaint about studios, sequels, executives, budgets, or the death of creativity.

Real Hollywood makes extraordinary things. It also cannot make everything.

Our freedom is more interesting than cynicism.

## 12. What we are not

Never Coming Soon is not:

- a screenwriting publication
- film criticism
- trade coverage
- a database of loglines
- parody by default
- a meme account
- fan fiction
- a technology publication
- a place to endlessly recast famous franchises
- a celebrity-casting exercise
- a collection of premise gimmicks

The audience encounters the finished creative work. Backstage tooling is not the product.

## 13. Originality

A Never Coming Soon concept must contain more than recombination.

"John Wick, but in Rome" is not enough.

"Succession at a hospital" is not enough.

Familiar genres and archetypes are welcome. Great entertainment frequently uses them.

But the concept needs its own character, relationship, world, dramatic engine, conflict, perspective, or execution.

References can orient. They cannot substitute for imagination.

## 14. Titles

Titles are part of the entertainment.

A strong title should feel plausible on a marquee, streaming homepage, awards ballot, or group chat.

It should fit the production and be memorable without sounding artificially clever.

A title does not need to explain the premise.

Working titles are always provisional until Generation decides otherwise.

## 15. Characters before celebrities

Characters must exist independently of casting.

The reader should understand who the person is before the actor arrives.

Casting should create recognition:

**Oh, that's perfect.**

Casting should deepen the production, not create the character retroactively.

## 16. Casting discipline

Movie stars are welcome. Less obvious performers are welcome.

The standard is fit.

We cast the character, not the marketing fantasy.

Repetition should be noticed. If the same handful of actors keep appearing, the imagination has narrowed.

## 17. Spoiler philosophy

Never Coming Soon sells the experience rather than exhaustively documenting the plot.

We reveal enough to make the production feel real and protect enough to preserve curiosity.

Default rule:

**Reveal the situation. Protect the payoff.**

Exceptions are allowed when the ending is essential to understanding why the concept works.

## 18. Internal canon

Every developed production should exist more fully internally than it does publicly.

For film, internal canon may include the full ending, character histories, connective beats, alternate choices, and scenes not included in the edition.

For television, internal canon may include complete season logic and possible future direction.

The public edition is a curated window, not the entire development file.

## 19. Future IP optionality

Never Coming Soon is a media publication first.

Audience, entertainment, and quality come first.

The work should not be compromised by speculation about adaptation.

But concepts should be developed carefully enough that one could someday travel further.

If a Never Coming Soon production eventually becomes real, that is the best possible punchline.

## 20. Production process

Never Coming Soon does not publicly frame its work around prompts, models, AI, generation, drafting systems, or production tooling.

We do not market technological novelty.

The audience receives entertainment, not a demonstration of how it was produced.

## 21. The enemy

Our greatest failure is not being insufficiently strange.

It is making something that feels manufactured rather than imagined.

Watch for:

- generic characters
- predictable twists
- interchangeable prose
- famous actors pasted onto thin roles
- vague scenes
- genre Mad Libs
- forced cleverness
- productions made weird merely to appear creative
- ideas that are exciting only as loglines

Specificity, human pull, and genuine desire matter more than novelty.

## 22. Final brand test

Before any production joins the catalog, ask:

- Would we actually watch this?
- Do we care what happens?
- Is there someone worth spending time with?
- Can we picture scenes?
- Does the genre work sincerely?
- Does the casting make it better?
- Does the idea have more underneath it than its hook?
- Does it feel meaningfully alive?
- Did we make it because we genuinely thought it would be awesome?

If the final answer is not obvious from the work, it is not ready.
