# Never Coming Soon
## Casting Standard v1.2

## 1. Purpose

Dream casting should make an already-developed Never Coming Soon production feel more real and more desirable.

Casting does not create the character.

The character exists first.

## 2. Core rule

**Character first. Actor second. Fit before fame.**

The desired reaction is:

**Oh, that's perfect.**

Not:

**Oh, I recognize that celebrity.**

## 3. What internal casting should solve

Casting should answer questions such as:

- What kind of warmth does this role need?
- How dangerous should the character feel before speaking?
- Does the role need comic timing, stillness, volatility, physicality, awkwardness, authority, vulnerability, or charm?
- What kind of chemistry must exist with another role?
- Does the audience need to trust the actor immediately or doubt them?
- Would a familiar screen persona help or hurt?

These are internal selection questions.

## 4. Avoid generic actor praise

Do not write:

- brings depth and nuance
- has incredible range
- is always compelling
- would crush this role
- has the gravitas needed

unless the statement is tied to specific character behavior or screen energy.

Casting copy should illuminate the production, not review the actor's career.

## 5. Comparison before selection

For major roles, the Casting Director should usually consider more than one plausible performer internally before selecting a preferred actor.

Alternatives are useful when they reveal different viable versions of the role.

Do not publish an endless list of options.

The final edition should feel decisive.

## 6. Chemistry

When a relationship is central, casting must consider the pair or group rather than each role in isolation.

A perfect individual actor can still be wrong for the ensemble.

Consider differences in rhythm, age, energy, status, physical presence, comic style, romantic chemistry, and emotional temperature.

## 7. Age and plausibility

Dream casting has flexibility, but obvious age, role, and physical mismatches should be intentional rather than accidental.

Do not rely on outdated assumptions about a performer's age or career stage when a simple current check would matter.

## 8. Fame

Movie stars are welcome.

Unknown or less famous actors are welcome.

Do not artificially avoid stars in pursuit of originality.

Do not repeatedly use the same famous performers because they are easy to imagine.

## 9. Catalog repetition

Casting history is memory, not a prohibition list.

The system should notice when the same actors recur across NCS productions.

Lead repetition deserves the most scrutiny because a lead face strongly defines a production in the reader's imagination.

If an actor already leads an approved Gold example or another very recent NCS production, ask whether an equally strong fresh choice exists before reusing them as another lead.

Supporting-role repetition is less consequential and may be perfectly fine.

Repetition is acceptable when the fit is genuinely exceptional.

Do not force novelty when the obvious actor is obviously right.

The goal is to prevent unconscious habit, not create a ban list.

## 10. Role size

Cast the roles that materially improve the reader's experience.

Do not cast every named person in the internal canon.

A typical film may need 3 to 6 dream-cast roles.

A series may need 4 to 7.

Use fewer when the production is intimate.

## 11. Public Cast completeness

Every paragraph inside the public THE CAST section must identify:

- the selected performer
- the character they play

If no performer was selected for a role, do not include a standalone Cast paragraph for that character.

A line such as `Denny reads exactly right because...` without naming who plays Denny is not a casting paragraph. Move the useful observation elsewhere or omit it.

Do not fill the gap by inventing a last-minute actor after the casting plan is already final unless the workflow explicitly reopens casting.

## 12. Public casting voice

The published Cast section should sound as though Never Coming Soon has already watched the performance.

Internal logic may say:

> Frank needs somebody who can balance charm and danger.

Published copy should say something closer to:

> Walton Goggins is Frank Mercer, and about five minutes in the whole thing makes sense.

Then describe what the imagined performance actually feels like on screen: a look, rhythm, pause, comic beat, physical habit, chemistry, stillness, volatility, warmth, or another specific behavior.

Prefer declarative language such as:

- X is Y
- X plays Y with...
- some of X's best moments are...
- their scenes become funnier when...

Avoid hypothetical public language such as:

- would bring
- could capture
- needs someone who
- would be perfect because

Those phrases expose the development process rather than preserving the public conceit.

## 13. Public casting length

Casting explanations should be concise enough that the section remains pleasurable rather than analytical.

The reader should immediately picture the actor behaving as the character.

Do not repeat the full character portrait. Add the face and performance.

## 14. Final test

Before locking a preferred cast, ask:

- Does this actor fit the actual person we developed?
- Does the choice improve the production or merely decorate it?
- Can I picture a specific scene with them?
- Does the ensemble make sense together?
- Are we repeating a recent NCS lead out of habit?
- If we are repeating one, is the fit clearly worth it?
- Would the reader immediately understand why this choice is fun?
- Can every public Cast paragraph name an actual selected performer?
