# Never Coming Soon
## Visual Constitution v1.0

## 1. Visual north star

Never Coming Soon should evoke **anticipation**.

The feeling of seeing a poster for something you have never heard of and immediately wondering when it comes out.

Then you realize it never will.

The brand should feel cinematic, cool, accessible, premium, fun, confident, and human.

It should not feel film-snobby, tech-forward, meme-like, artificially nostalgic, or streaming-platform corporate.

## 2. Governing visual idea

**The production owns the fantasy. Never Coming Soon owns the frame.**

Every movie or show can have its own visual universe.

Never Coming Soon provides the recognizable studio around it.

## 3. Core brand palette

### Midnight Navy

`#08192F`

Primary dark brand surface.

### Warm Ivory

`#F5EEDF`

Primary light surface.

### Reel Orange

`#F24B2C`

Primary signal color.

Use as punctuation, not saturation.

### Electric Cobalt

`#315CFF`

Secondary accent used sparingly.

### Slate

`#202632`

Secondary dark neutral.

## 4. Palette hierarchy

Directional usage across house-branded surfaces:

- Midnight Navy: roughly 50 percent
- Warm Ivory: roughly 35 percent
- Reel Orange: roughly 10 percent
- Electric Cobalt: roughly 5 percent

The productions themselves may use any colors appropriate to their worlds.

## 5. Typography

### House display

Archivo Narrow, Bold or ExtraBold.

Use for large hooks, metadata moments, studio labels, and house typography when the production does not require a bespoke title treatment.

### House sans

Archivo, Regular through Bold.

Use for metadata, navigation, labels, captions, names, and functional information.

### Editorial serif

Newsreader, Regular through Medium, including italics.

Use for body prose, character descriptions, episode descriptions, pull quotes, and narrative copy.

## 6. Logo

The approved primary logo is the custom retro-cinematic Never Coming Soon lockup using navy, orange, and cream.

The logo artwork is treated as a fixed brand asset. Do not attempt to recreate it from a standard typeface.

Required practical variants:

- horizontal with tagline
- horizontal without tagline
- square / stacked with tagline
- square / stacked without tagline
- compact NCS mark
- dark-background treatment
- cream-background treatment
- one-color navy
- one-color cream / white

## 7. Production title treatment

The production's title is allowed to have its own typography and visual identity.

Never Coming Soon should not force house typography onto every fictional production.

The production owns its title treatment. NCS signs the work.

## 8. Image philosophy

The standard is:

**Real advertising art for an unreal production.**

Not generic concept art.

Not imagery whose primary novelty is that it can be generated.

The medium can be photographic, typographic, illustrated, painted, graphic, minimal, collage, surreal, documentary-style, or animated.

The movie chooses.

## 9. Carousel modes

A carousel may combine four visual modes.

### Cinema

Full-bleed imagery, minimal copy, maximum immersion.

### Editorial

Navy or cream surfaces, strong typography, prose, breathing room.

### Artifact

Something that appears to exist inside the story world, such as a newspaper, scoreboard, menu, invitation, document, map, receipt, campaign poster, or magazine.

### Studio

Never Coming Soon's own visual language, such as casting cards, episode guides, metadata, and end cards.

## 10. Visual rhythm

Do not make every slide equally loud.

Use contrast between imagery, quiet editorial pages, artifacts, and house cards.

Visual rhythm should mirror prose rhythm.

## 11. Character imagery

Default rule:

**Words create the person. Casting supplies the face. Imagery creates the world.**

Do not create mandatory character portraits.

## 12. End card

The end card should become one of the brand's most recognizable surfaces.

Preferred structure:

- Midnight Navy background
- small Reel Orange NCS mark
- large Warm Ivory typography
- a recurring line such as "UNFORTUNATELY, YOU CAN'T WATCH IT."
- secondary copy such as "You can read the whole thing."
- slogan and CTA

## 13. Avoid cinema cliches

Do not rely on constant film reels, sprocket holes, clapperboards, projector icons, popcorn, red curtains, gold-award aesthetics, scratches, or fake vintage film textures.

The fact that the brand is about movies should come from the movies.

## 14. Final visual test

Ask:

- Does this feel cinematic?
- Does this feel contemporary?
- Does the production still have its own personality?
- Is NCS recognizable without dominating?
- Is there enough breathing room?
- Does this make the imaginary production feel more real?

If not, simplify.
